Hardware Concept Guide

This guide maps each feature of the browser demo to a real hardware idea. It is a concept guide, not a build manual. This website does not connect to any hardware.

Demo only. This is not a child safety device. Never leave a child alone in a car.

Safety First (Read Before You Build Anything)

  • Real power windows need anti-pinch protection. A DIY build must never bypass it. A voice system must never be the only layer that stops a window from closing on a hand, a neck, or a child.
  • Do not connect anything to airbags, to the CAN bus safety circuits, or to the immobilizer. Do not wire into the car lock or window circuits without a qualified auto electrician.
  • This project makes no safety claim. Do not say that this demo or a student prototype meets any vehicle safety standard.
  • A child presence radar can miss a sleeping child. A BLE distance reading is a rough guess. Neither one replaces a person.

1. What Each Demo Feature Maps To

In the browser demo, every feature runs as code inside Chrome. Chrome can send microphone audio to Google for speech recognition, and Mode B sends recognized text (never audio) to Groq. The table shows the real idea behind each feature.

Demo Feature What the Demo Does Real Hardware Idea
Voice windows Mode A coded words or Mode B Groq rules move a window to 0%, 50%, or 100%. A voice brain sends a short command to a bridge board. The car window controller must keep its own anti-pinch logic.
Speaker lock A voiceprint vector in the browser accepts or rejects a speaker. A speaker model on a phone or laptop. It is a demo lock, because a recording or a similar voice can pass.
Keyfob Buttons lock, unlock, and move all windows. A toggle says whether the keyfob is carried. A key or a phone that the car recognizes. Check the maker documentation of any real keyless system before you copy its method (not verified here).
Kick sensor A kick zone opens a door. A locked car needs the keyfob. A foot-motion sensor, such as a capacitive or radar sensor, that only acts when a valid key is near.
Child alert A simulated sensor and a simulated owner distance start a 60 s countdown, a chime, and a move of the windows to 50%. A presence radar in the cabin plus a distance estimate for the owner. See the limits in section 3.

2. Architecture

  • Voice brain: A phone or laptop runs speech recognition, the speaker check, and the rules (this demo). It sends only the final command.
  • ESP32 bridge: A small ESP32 board receives the command over Wi-Fi or BLE. It drives indicators on a bench model. It must not drive a real car window motor.
  • Keyfob distance: The ESP32 can read the BLE signal strength (RSSI) of a phone or tag. It turns the reading into a rough distance.
  • Child presence radar: A small millimeter-wave presence module reports "something moves here" to the ESP32.

Use a bench model with LEDs or small indicator motors that are not connected to a car. Test every rule there first.

3. Limits You Must State to Your Examiners

  • BLE signal strength gives a rough distance only. Bodies, bags, metal, and walls change the reading, so treat any distance as a rough guess.
  • A radar can miss a sleeping child. A still, sleeping child can read as empty. The alert never replaces a person.
  • The 60 s countdown and the 50% window move are demo rules. They are not tested, and they do not make a car safe for a child.
  • The voice lock is a demo. A recording of the enrolled speaker can pass. Similar voices can pass.

5. Wiring Caution

  • Keep the ESP32 on a bench with USB power. Do not connect it to a car battery, a car window motor, or a car lock.
  • A car uses a 12 V system. Check the datasheet of every board for its input limits before you connect it (not verified here).
  • Never splice into airbag wiring, CAN bus safety circuits, or the immobilizer. A fault there can injure people and can stop the car from starting.
  • If a real car modification is part of your project, ask a qualified auto electrician. Keep the factory anti-pinch logic and the factory window switches working.
  • Test with a multimeter and LEDs before you connect anything to a motor.

6. FAQ

Does this website control a real car?

No. Everything runs in your browser. No hardware is controlled.

Can I use the child alert in my car?

No. It is a demo. Never leave a child alone in a car, and do not trust a DIY alert to protect one.

Why does the alert keep the windows at 50%?

It is a demo rule that shows ventilation. It is not a tested safety measure.

Why does the keyfob toggle exist if the car is locked?

The kick sensor needs it. A locked car opens a door on a kick only when the keyfob is carried.

Which side is the driver?

Malaysia is right-hand drive. The driver window is front right and the passenger window is front left.
